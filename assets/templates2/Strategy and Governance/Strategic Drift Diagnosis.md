# Strategic Drift Diagnosis

## Problem type

The user says something like "we have a strategy but nothing seems to be moving," "everyone's busy but we're not actually getting anywhere," or "we keep changing direction and I don't know why we're stuck." This isn't a request to *produce* a strategy document (that's v1's SWOT or Market Entry Strategy) — it's a request to explain why an existing strategy isn't translating into results.

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the stated strategy or top priority right now, in the user's own words?
- What are people actually spending their time and budget on day to day — does it match the stated priority?
- How long has this been the strategy, and has it changed recently or often?
- Is there one clear decision-maker for trade-offs, or do different people pull in different directions?
- What does "not moving" actually look like — no measurable progress, progress that doesn't matter, or disagreement about what progress even means?

## Methodology

1. **Compare stated strategy to actual behavior.** Drift usually shows up as a gap between what leadership says matters and where time/money actually goes — find that gap concretely, not abstractly.
2. **Rule out the common mechanisms**: unclear ownership of the priority, too many competing priorities diluting focus, a strategy that was never translated into anything operational, or a strategy that's stale relative to what's actually happened in the market.
3. **Identify which mechanism is actually dominant** — a company with five equally-weighted priorities has a different problem than one with one clear priority nobody's executing.
4. **Reason through what would actually close the gap** — this is rarely "write a better strategy document" and usually "make the existing priority operational" (who owns it, what changes this quarter, what stops).
5. **State the trade-off** — focusing on one priority means explicitly deprioritizing something else; name what that is.

## Connects to (v1)

If the diagnosis reveals the strategy itself (not just execution) is the problem, produce a [SWOT Analysis and Action Plan](../../templates/Strategy and Governance/SWOT Analysis and Action Plan.md) or refresh the [Competitive Landscape Analysis](../../templates/Strategy and Governance/Competitive Landscape Analysis.md) as the next real step. If the strategy itself is sound and the gap is purely operational, produce an [Executive Summary Builder](../../templates/Strategy and Governance/Executive Summary Builder.md) reframing the priority in a form that's actually actionable, rather than a fresh strategy document restating what's already agreed.

## Deliverable

1. **The actual gap** — stated priority vs. actual behavior, concretely
2. **The dominant mechanism** — which of the common causes is really driving it
3. **What would close it** — specific, operational, not another planning document unless the strategy itself is the problem
4. **The trade-off** — what gets explicitly deprioritized
5. **The connected v1 output** — whichever of the above actually fits

## Requirements

- Don't default to "you need a clearer strategy" if the diagnosis actually points to an execution or ownership gap
- Name the specific deprioritization the fix requires — a recommendation that doesn't cost anything usually isn't a real fix for drift
- If the user can't articulate the stated strategy at all, that itself is diagnostic information, not a gap to fill in on their behalf
