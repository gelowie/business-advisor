# Funding Decision

## Problem type

The business needs, or is considering, outside capital — a loan, credit line, lease, investor money, crowdfunding, a grant — and must decide **whether** to raise, **how much**, **from what source**, and **on what terms**. Read `references/financial-core.md` §10, `references/finance-fundraising.md` and `references/jurisdiction.md`.

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the money for (working capital, equipment, growth, acquisition, survival), and what return or relief does it produce?
- How much is needed, by when, and how certain is the amount?
- Current cash flow: can it service debt (DSCR)? How predictable is it?
- Collateral available; owner willingness to give personal guarantees, equity or board seats?
- Growth ambition: steady business or venture-scale?
- Jurisdiction (entity, state/country) and the owner's personal financial exposure?

## Methodology

1. **Size the need with a real number:** the funding gap from the forecast (peak cumulative shortfall plus a buffer of roughly 60–90 days of fixed costs), not a round guess. State what happens if the money isn't raised.
2. **Check cheaper capital first:** internal fixes (working-capital release, cost cuts), supplier or customer financing (terms, deposits), retained cash.
3. **Match source to purpose and risk profile:**
   - Stable cash flow plus collateral → term loan, equipment financing, line of credit.
   - Seasonal or timing gap → line of credit or receivables financing.
   - Uncertain, high-growth, asset-light → angel/venture equity, SAFEs, convertible notes.
   - Consumer product with a story → crowdfunding (also a demand test).
   - Mission-aligned or R&D → grants (slow, restricted; apply early).
   - Cash-flow-positive, no desire to dilute → revenue-based financing.
4. **Compute the all-in cost and the downside for each option:** interest and fees → effective annual rate; debt service vs cash flow (DSCR) at base and −20% revenue; personal guarantee exposure; dilution, liquidation preferences and control for equity (use post-money math); what happens to the owner's take-home and ownership.
5. **Compare options in a table** on cost, speed, certainty, control surrendered, downside risk, reversibility, and fit — without picking early.
6. **Recommend with the reasoning and the conditions under which the recommendation flips** (e.g. "if revenue falls 25%, the loan breaches the coverage covenant").
7. **List the legal and tax questions** the chosen route raises (securities exemptions, entity structure, guarantees) and who to ask; verify current rules per `jurisdiction.md`.
8. **Give a preparation checklist:** documents lenders or investors will ask for, the numbers they'll test, and the timeline.

## Deliverable

Funding-gap size; option comparison table with all-in cost and downside case; recommendation with flip conditions; legal/tax question list; preparation checklist.

## Requirements

- Funding need is derived from the forecast, not guessed
- Every option shows effective cost, debt-service or dilution effect, and downside
- Personal guarantees and control terms are stated explicitly
- Rates, caps and exemptions are verified, not quoted from memory
- Not presented as legal, tax or investment advice
