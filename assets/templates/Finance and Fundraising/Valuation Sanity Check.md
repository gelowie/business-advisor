# Valuation Sanity Check

## Summary

Produces a rough, defensible value range for a business using at least two methods and shows what moves it, so the user can sanity-check an offer, a price or an equity split. It is not a formal appraisal.

## Inputs

- **Last 2–3 years of revenue and profit** — [and whether the owner's pay and one-off items are already adjusted]
- **Owner's role** — [hours per week, and whether the business runs without them]
- **Revenue profile** — [recurring share, top-customer concentration, growth rate]
- **Assets and debt** — [major assets, loans]
- **Purpose** — [selling, raising equity, buy-out, succession]
- **Business type** — [service, digital, physical, local]

## Deliverable

1. Normalized earnings (owner's market salary applied, one-offs removed)
2. Value range from two or more methods that suit the business type, with the assumptions behind each
3. A sensitivity table showing how the range moves with the multiple and with earnings
4. Value adjustments up or down (owner dependence, concentration, recurring revenue, records, scale)
5. A single-sentence conclusion: where a reasonable price sits and what would change it
6. The top three actions that would raise value

## Requirements

- Present a range, never one number
- Cite and date any multiples or comparable sales; label them unverified if they cannot be checked
- Apply owner-dependence and concentration adjustments explicitly
- State plainly that this is not a formal valuation, and that tax and deal structure need a professional
