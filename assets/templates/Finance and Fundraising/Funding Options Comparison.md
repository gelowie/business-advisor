# Funding Options Comparison

## Summary

Compares the realistic ways to fund a specific need — loan, line of credit, equipment financing, investor equity, crowdfunding, revenue-based financing, grants, supplier or customer financing — on cost, speed, control and downside, using the business's own numbers.

## Inputs

- **Amount needed and purpose** — [what the money does and the expected return or relief]
- **Timing** — [when the money is needed and how firm that date is]
- **Cash flow** — [average monthly net cash flow, how stable it is]
- **Collateral and guarantees** — [assets available; willingness to sign personal guarantees]
- **Ownership goals** — [how much equity or control the owner will give up]
- **Jurisdiction and entity** — [country, state, structure]

## Deliverable

1. Gap sizing: the amount needed with a buffer, and what happens if it isn't raised
2. Comparison table of 3–5 realistic options: effective annual cost (interest plus fees, or dilution), monthly payment or ownership given up, speed, certainty, control or guarantees surrendered, and downside if revenue drops 20%
3. Debt-service check: coverage of payments by cash flow in the base and downside cases
4. A recommendation, with the conditions under which it would change
5. The legal and tax questions the chosen route raises, and who should answer them
6. A short preparation checklist (documents and numbers the funder will ask for)

## Requirements

- Compute cost as an effective annual rate, not just the quoted rate
- Show downside for every option; don't show only the best case
- State personal-guarantee and control terms explicitly
- Verify current rates, caps and exemptions; label anything unverified
- State that this is not legal, tax or investment advice
