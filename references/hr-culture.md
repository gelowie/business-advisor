# Human Resources & Organizational Culture

## Talent Acquisition

- **Job description writing:** Lead with what the person will actually do and own in the first 90 days, not a generic responsibilities list — specificity attracts candidates who can picture the role and repels those who can't. Separate "must-have" from "nice-to-have" qualifications honestly; overloaded requirement lists shrink the applicant pool without improving quality.
- **Structured interview scoring rubrics:** Define the 3-5 competencies that actually predict success in this specific role, write a concrete question per competency, and score every candidate against the same rubric independently before discussing as a panel — this reduces halo effect and recency bias compared to unstructured "vibe" interviews.
- **Onboarding playbooks:** Structure around the first day, first week, first 30/60/90 days, each with clear goals — what the new hire should understand, who they should have met, and what they should have accomplished by each checkpoint.

## Performance Management

- **360-degree review frameworks:** Combine self, manager, peer, and (where relevant) direct-report feedback — but weight manager input most heavily for performance/comp decisions, since peer feedback is more useful for development than for high-stakes judgment calls.
- **Compensation & benefits strategy:** Decide where the business wants to sit relative to market (lead, match, or lag) deliberately, and be consistent about it across roles — inconsistent positioning (overpaying one role while underpaying a comparable one) is a common driver of internal pay-equity complaints.
- **Employee retention plans:** Exit interviews and stay interviews (asking current high performers what would make them leave) both matter — most retention risk is visible before someone actually resigns, if anyone's asking.

## Legal HR

- **Workplace policy drafting:** Code of conduct and remote work agreements should be specific enough to actually guide a real decision (e.g. "core hours are X-Y" not "be available during business hours") while leaving room for reasonable judgment on edge cases.
- **Conflict resolution protocols:** Define a clear escalation path (direct conversation first, then manager involvement, then HR/formal process) and put it in writing before conflict happens — improvised conflict resolution in the moment tends to feel arbitrary to the people involved.
