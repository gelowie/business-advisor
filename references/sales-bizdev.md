# Sales & Business Development

## Pipeline Management

- **Sales funnel design:** Define clear, observable stage-exit criteria for every stage (e.g. "Qualified" means they've confirmed budget, authority, need, and timeline — not just "we had a good call"). Funnels with vague stage definitions produce unreliable forecasts because everyone interprets "stage 2" differently.
- **Lead scoring:** Score on both fit (does this prospect match the ideal customer profile — size, industry, budget) and engagement/intent (have they taken high-signal actions — requested a demo, visited pricing page, opened multiple emails). Fit without intent is a cold lead; intent without fit is a bad-fit lead that will churn even if it closes.
- **CRM workflow setup:** Map the CRM stages to the funnel definition above exactly — a CRM with stages that don't match how the team actually sells becomes a data-entry chore no one trusts, and forecasts drift from reality.

## High-Ticket Sales

- **B2B discovery scripts:** Structure around understanding the prospect's current situation, the specific pain/cost of the status quo, what they've already tried, and what a good outcome looks like to them — in that order. Resist pitching the solution until the pain is clearly established and, ideally, quantified by the prospect themselves.
- **Handling objections:** Acknowledge first, then clarify the real concern behind the stated one (price objections are often actually value or trust objections), then address with a specific proof point (case study, guarantee, pilot option) — don't just argue the stated objection down.
- **Proposal writing:** Lead with the prospect's stated problem and desired outcome in their own language, then the proposed approach, then pricing tied clearly to the value/outcome (not just a line-item cost breakdown), then a clear next step with a date.

## Partnerships

- **Affiliate program design:** Commission structure should be generous enough to motivate real promotion effort but sustainable against margin — model it against actual gross margin, not revenue, or the program can become unprofitable at scale.
- **Joint venture (JV) structuring:** Clarify upfront, in writing, before any work starts: who contributes what (capital, audience, product, expertise), how revenue/profit splits, who owns resulting IP, and the exit/dissolution terms if it doesn't work out. Most JV conflicts trace back to one of these being left informal.
- **Channel partner strategy:** Define what's in it for the partner as concretely as for the end customer (margin, exclusivity, co-marketing support, lead sharing) — a channel strategy that only benefits the vendor rarely gets real partner effort.
